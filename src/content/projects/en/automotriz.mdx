## The problem

**Real Motors** was running on **Jumpseller**: a slow storefront, not fully responsive, with poor SEO. I did the analysis myself: I talked to the owners and users, reviewed the platform end to end, and looked at the budget. The real challenge was behind the scenes: inventory with no traceability, sales on paper, reservations with no follow-up, and a workshop that depended on a whiteboard.

Inventory meant creating a vehicle or parts listing and adding stock, but **sales never decremented it**: there was no traceability. Sales notes were filled in by hand on paper. Work orders were written on a whiteboard with a marker — sometimes not even that. The workshop had no record of the costs put into each vehicle. A reservation was a label on the Jumpseller product, with no real follow-up.

The owners had not asked for a sales-notes module. I proposed it: without a sales document, the rest of the system does not close.

## What I built

An operations ERP in production at [realmotors.cl](https://www.realmotors.cl) that **replaced Jumpseller as the business's operating platform**. An internal panel with users and roles, plus a public catalog for customers.

- **Vehicle catalog:** the face of the business, built for performance, mobile, and SEO.
- **Inventory as two models:** vehicles as unique units (VIN) and parts/accessories as SKUs with quantity, low-stock alerts, search, and CSV import/export.
- **Sales notes** with installment payments (*abonos*) on the same note, in CLP and USD.
- **Reservations** with real follow-up, not a label on the listing.
- **Work orders** with costs and priority, instead of the whiteboard.
- **Quick WhatsApp quote** with the import breakdown.

Out of scope on purpose: this is not accounting or SII invoicing; invoices are issued only externally, as required by Zofri regulations.

## Technical decisions

A **modular monolith** to keep operating cost down: one deploy, with inventory, sales, reservations, and work orders as separate domains, so they can be split later if the budget allows. **Java + Spring Boot** API on Railway over **PostgreSQL**, JWT auth, and persistence with JPA + Flyway.

A **React + TypeScript SPA** on Cloudflare Pages instead of Jumpseller templates: the catalog and the panel load as an app, not as a generic storefront.

## The hard problem

A vehicle is not a SKU. Mixing VIN (one unit = one car) with accessories (quantity that goes up and down) is the usual failure of a generic ERP. Inventory has to treat them differently, and both still have to land on the same sales note.

The commercial knot was payment. Amounts are large: the customer almost never pays in full the same day. A note can accumulate **up to 10 installments, in CLP and USD**, on the same document. The note balance is the source of truth; there is no loose receipt that drifts from the paper copy.

## The outcome

In daily use by 14+ people, over an inventory of 200+ vehicles and 300+ accessories. Stock decrements on sale, with traceability by user and customer. Sales notes, work orders, and workshop costs are digital: search and create are faster, priorities are visible in the system, and the public catalog is the business site — Jumpseller is gone.
